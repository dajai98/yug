## Inputs required before a human ceremony

The governance owner must record the independent reviewer's identity **or** explicitly select `SOLO_CUSTODIAN_TWO_STEP_REVIEW` with `independent_reviewer=NOT_APPLICABLE_SOLO_OWNER`; the offline signing device and approved OpenSSL build; the primary private-key storage location outside `C:\YUG\yug1`; two physically separate encrypted backup locations; two independent, separately retained channels for the public-key fingerprint and exact trust-pin identity; exact UTC validity start and end; and the chosen TSA or `TIMESTAMP_PENDING`. The proposed first interval is two years, but no interval starts and no pin becomes `ACTIVE` until its exact dates and governance approval are recorded. The custodian is Rai. Do not send a private-key passphrase to YUG, Codex, ChatGPT, a reviewer, a script, or any evidence record.

For the private, solo-owned YUG system, `YUG-EVIDENCE-KEY-002` may use the explicit solo model. This is **not** a claim of independent human review. The owner must approve a canonical pre-generation decision **before** generation, then record a separate second self-review after generation and **before** a distinct activation decision. The second review must recompute the exact public PEM SHA256, confirm Ed25519 and key ID, verify both encrypted backups by restore → derive public key → compare the full SHA256 fingerprint, compare two independently retained trust-pin identities with the canonical record, and check the intended UTC interval and current key status. Preserve both review records and all status transitions as immutable audit history with externally retained identities. A mismatch, missing evidence, stale pin, missing timestamp under the production acceptance policy, or absent activation decision fails closed. The key and its trust pin grant no source, intended-use, corpus, or training authority. `YUG-EVIDENCE-KEY-001` cannot receive retroactive pre-generation approval or become an active production root.

## Roles and authority

| Object or role | Function | Trust boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Production Ed25519 private key | Signs the domain-separated manifest identity on a dedicated offline signing system | Secret, kept outside the repository, bundle, corpus artifacts, and application runtime. Possession alone does not approve content. |
| Public verification key | Exact PEM bytes used by the offline verifier | Public but not self-authenticating; its SHA256 must match an independently approved trust pin. |
| Trust pin/root | Canonical `yug-evidence-trust-pin-v1` record naming key ID, PEM hash and path, status, interval, predecessor, governance identity, and its own identity | The exact pin identity must arrive through an independent trusted channel. A copy beside a bundle has no authority by itself. |
| Governance owner and approval record | Authorizes key creation, activation, rotation, status changes, and custodians; a separate source decision authorizes intended use | The current verifier checks only the governance-decision identity's *shape*, not the referenced record or the authority of its reviewer. Operators must independently check and pin the governance record before activating the trust pin. |
| Detached signature | `yug-evidence-signature-v2`, containing `key_id`, manifest identity, exact PEM hash and Ed25519 signature | Proves the holder of the private key signed `YUG-EVIDENCE-MANIFEST-V1\0` followed by the ASCII manifest identity. New signatures use v2; the verifier still accepts legacy v1 with a pinned key for compatibility. |
| Timestamp trust anchor | Independently chosen RFC 3161 TSA CA, accepted policy and archival validation record | A TSA response cannot authenticate the YUG key, grant content rights, or establish an earlier acquisition date. The anchor is retained; no production response has been requested. |

The operator supplies the externally trusted **manifest identity** and **current trust-pin identity** separately from mutable bundle storage. The production verifier returns `authority=NONE`; production admission, rights, intended use, governance freshness, and accounting remain separate gates. A static `ACTIVE` pin cannot reveal a later revocation by itself. Before each new signing or production verification, the operator must obtain the current key-status/pin identity through the independent governance channel. Missing or disagreeing current channel records block verification.

Public PEM + trust pin + signature + bundle stored together in one mutable directory cannot establish trust. At verification, the operator obtains the expected **current** trust-pin identity from the independent governance channel, checks agreement with the second retained channel, and supplies that exact `sha256:` value as `--expected-trust-pin-identity`. `training.evidence_trust.verify_trust_pin` rejects noncanonical or duplicate-field pin JSON, recomputes its canonical identity, compares it to that external value, hashes the actual public PEM bytes and compares the fingerprint, and checks key status and active UTC interval. If either external channel disagrees or the local identity differs, stop; do not choose a local replacement pin. The manifest identity needs its own independent pin. The verifier's `authority=NONE` result is never source or training approval.

## Future key creation and activation

1. The YUG governance owner approves a named key ID, the signing purpose, authorized custodian and either an independent fingerprint reviewer or the explicit solo review model, offline device, OpenSSL version/provider, planned validity interval, and backup plan **before** generation. Record the approval outside bundle storage. Do not reuse development keys or IDs. A previously generated key cannot be qualified by a later pre-generation approval.
2. The custodian boots a controlled offline device, verifies the approved OpenSSL installation and OS cryptographic random-number source, and generates one Ed25519 key with `openssl genpkey -algorithm ED25519` into an encrypted PKCS#8 PEM file on an encrypted offline volume. Use OpenSSL's interactive encryption passphrase handling; do not place a passphrase in command arguments, environment variables, shell history, or logs. If the approved tool cannot encrypt the output safely, stop and choose an approved OS-protected or hardware-backed key store. No YUG application code generates or accepts a private-key path.
3. Export only the SubjectPublicKeyInfo PEM with `openssl pkey -pubout`. Fix its exact bytes before pinning: `public_key_sha256` is SHA256 of the PEM file, **not** of decoded key material or a visually copied fingerprint. Record the key ID, algorithm, PEM byte length/hash, OpenSSL build, device/custodian, UTC creation time, and generation approval. The private key remains offline.
4. Under the independent-reviewer model, a separate reviewer obtains the exported public PEM through a second controlled channel, recomputes its SHA256, compares the entire 64-hex-character digest and key ID with the custodian's record, and checks that the public key is Ed25519. Under `SOLO_CUSTODIAN_TWO_STEP_REVIEW`, Rai performs the same checks in a separately recorded second self-review after generation, including backup restore/fingerprint checks and the two external pin channels; do not represent this as independent human verification. A mismatch stops activation. The reviewer or second self-review also checks that the governance record's purpose, validity dates and custodian match the prior approval.
5. After review, prepare a non-operational trust-pin candidate with exact PEM hash, relative `public_key_path`, validity dates, predecessor and governance identity. A candidate is not an `ACTIVE` runtime pin. Check its canonical bytes and recomputed content identity; `training.evidence_trust.verify_trust_pin` applies only to a later externally pinned runtime pin with a supported status. A self-seal or local copy is not approval. Independently retain the exact trust-pin identity and public PEM fingerprint in **both** (a) an offline, dated operator record and (b) a separate access-controlled governance record held away from the bundle and signing device. Independently retain the current status and any replacement-pin identity in the same channels. This design step itself did not materialize a trust pin; the later KEY-002 status is recorded above.
6. The governance owner makes a **separate activation decision** only after the second review and both pin channels agree. Only then may a canonical v1 pin with `status=ACTIVE`, exact UTC interval, and the activation governance decision identity be externally released and used for current verification. The signing custodian may then sign only a separately reviewed canonical **production-mode** manifest whose identity is independently approved and whose mandatory RFC 3161 timestamp requirement is enforced at the production acceptance gate. Save the detached v2 signature and verify it offline against the current external pins before publication. The signer must not decide source rights, intended use, or admission.

The future offline device's proposed OpenSSL 3.x invocation is below. The angle-bracketed paths are placeholders to replace with approved absolute paths **outside this repository**. Keep the quotes around each path so spaces are safe. Run these commands only in an interactive offline terminal after the tool build, encrypted PKCS#8 output, and passphrase prompt have been rehearsed with test-only keys and approved. A missing prompt or a private-key output that is not encrypted PKCS#8 is a stop condition. Never add `-text`, omit `-out`, pass a secret through `-pass`/`-passin`, or redirect private-key bytes to a console or log.

```text
openssl genpkey -algorithm ED25519 -aes-256-cbc -outform PEM -out "<OFFLINE_PRIVATE_KEY_PATH>"
openssl pkey -in "<OFFLINE_PRIVATE_KEY_PATH>" -pubout -outform PEM -out "<PUBLIC_KEY_EXPORT_PATH>"
openssl pkey -pubin -in "<PUBLIC_KEY_EXPORT_PATH>" -pubcheck -noout
openssl pkey -in "<OFFLINE_PRIVATE_KEY_PATH>" -pubout -outform PEM -out "<OFFLINE_DERIVED_PUBLIC_KEY_PATH>"
```

On a Windows review station compute the exact exported PEM fingerprint with `Get-FileHash -Algorithm SHA256 -LiteralPath "<PUBLIC_KEY_EXPORT_PATH>"`; on a Unix-like review station use `sha256sum "<PUBLIC_KEY_EXPORT_PATH>"`. The reviewer independently checks that the temporary derived public PEM has the same complete SHA256 and that the exported public key is Ed25519. Pin the **saved exported PEM bytes**; re-exporting under another OpenSSL build or rewriting line endings is not a substitute for byte comparison. Preserve a byte-identical public copy through the independent review channel. These are future ceremony instructions, **not commands executed for this design task**.

For the future ceremony, the reviewer can verify the exported public key and private/public pairing without printing private bytes. All paths below remain **outside the repository**; the second public export goes to a separate temporary location on the offline device. OpenSSL prompts for the private-key passphrase interactively. The reviewer compares both complete SHA256 values, not a shortened fingerprint:

```text
Get-FileHash -Algorithm SHA256 -LiteralPath "<PUBLIC_KEY_EXPORT_PATH>"
Get-FileHash -Algorithm SHA256 -LiteralPath "<OFFLINE_DERIVED_PUBLIC_KEY_PATH>"
```

The matching public PEM SHA256, approved key ID, governance decision identity, relative public-key reference, activation interval, predecessor `NONE`, and approved status are the inputs for a future canonical trust-pin record. Computing that record's local identity is not activation: its exact identity must be independently reviewed and retained in both external channels. Do not put the private PEM or its hash in any trust pin or bundle. Remove the temporary derived public copy after verification under the approved ceremony procedure.

OpenSSL documents Ed25519 generation through [`genpkey`](https://docs.openssl.org/master/man1/openssl-genpkey/) and one-shot raw signing through [`pkeyutl`](https://docs.openssl.org/3.0/man1/openssl-pkeyutl/). The exact commands, provider and encryption option must be rehearsed on **test-only** keys, then approved for the production device; this document is not an instruction to run the ceremony now. Never print or hash the private PEM into a YUG evidence artifact.

## Storage, backup and recovery

**Current practical baseline:** a dedicated offline, full-disk-encrypted device or removable encrypted volume, with a separately encrypted private PKCS#8 PEM and a strong passphrase known only to the custodian. Bring the device online only through a future separately approved transfer/signing process; prefer moving reviewed manifest identities in and signatures out. Limit OS accounts and physical access. Do not store the private key in Git, `artifacts/`, the public-key root, cloud sync, a CI secret, or the corpus ledger.

Use separate conceptual areas: **private ceremony storage** for `<OFFLINE_PRIVATE_KEY_PATH>`, `<BACKUP_A_PATH>`, `<BACKUP_B_PATH>` and private operational notes, all outside `C:\YUG\yug1`; and **public/governance evidence** for `<PUBLIC_KEY_EXPORT_PATH>`, the public PEM SHA256, key ID, approval and reviewer records, trust pin, and timestamp evidence. No actual physical path has been assigned. Never copy an encrypted or decrypted private key into the public area. The two backup media must be physically separate from one another and from the primary.

Create **two** encrypted backup copies on separate offline media in separate physical locations before activation. Use distinct media encryption/passphrases or independently wrapped keys, record copy identifiers, creation dates, encryption/tool versions, custodians, tamper seals, and an inventory of the public key/pin identities. Never package the decryption passphrase, private key, backup media, and the sole trust-pin record together. Never back up raw corpus, credentials unrelated to signing, or unsigned approval material beside the key. At creation and periodically (for example, annually), perform an isolated restore test: decrypt a backup on a controlled offline device, derive the public key, compare its exact PEM fingerprint to the externally pinned one, sign a **test-only** message, verify it with the public key, then securely remove the temporary recovered copy. Log the test without exporting secret bytes. If restoration or custody cannot be verified, suspend signing and rotate under governance control. Suspected exposure of any backup is a suspected private-key compromise.

Test **each** backup independently before recording `backup_verification_status=VERIFIED`: recover it only onto an isolated offline device, interactively unlock it, derive a temporary public PEM without printing the private key, compare the full SHA256 with the independently retained production public-key fingerprint, and verify a test-only signature. Destroy temporary recovered private and derived-public copies under the approved procedure; record media ID, date, reviewer, pass/fail and public fingerprint only. Do not perform this test now.

**Future high-assurance upgrade:** move generation and signing to a non-exportable hardware key or HSM with documented Ed25519 support, access control and two-person activation. Keep the same manifest message and externally pinned public-key model; issue a new key ID and trust pin. Hardware migration does not rewrite old bundles or make past file-backed signatures stronger retroactively. Backup and recovery then follow the hardware vendor's protected key or disaster-recovery procedure, with an independent restore test.

## Lifecycle and rotation

`CREATED` and `DESTROYED` are governance-event states, **not** accepted v1 trust-pin statuses. The verifier accepts only `ACTIVE`, `RETIRED`, `REVOKED`, and `COMPROMISED`; it fails closed on other values. Every status transition requires a new externally approved governance event and, where the public pin status changes, a new canonical pin identity that is published through the two independent channels. An old `ACTIVE` pin must not be used as a current-status assertion after a transition.

| State | New signatures | Current bundle acceptance | Historical verification |
|---|---|---|---|
| CREATED | Forbidden until activation | Forbidden | None. No active trust pin exists. |
| ACTIVE | Allowed only within the approved interval and separate manifest approval | Signature may verify, but verifier still grants no content authority | Cryptographic verification is possible against the retained pin; signing time is not inferred from a signature alone. |
| RETIRED | Forbidden | Forbidden for new/current authority | Explicit archival-integrity mode may return `VERIFIED_RETIRED_ARCHIVAL_ONLY`; retain old pin, key, signature and independently established chronology. |
| REVOKED | Forbidden | Forbidden | Current verifier rejects even archival verification. Any narrower historical claim requires independently established reason and time plus a separately approved process. |
| COMPROMISED | Forbidden | Forbidden | Current verifier rejects even archival verification. Do not assert pre-compromise validity without independent trusted chronology and a future reviewed policy. |
| DESTROYED | Forbidden | Forbidden | Destruction applies to private material. Public key, pins, signatures, governance records and archival evidence remain preserved; acceptance follows the last externally trusted `RETIRED`, `REVOKED`, or `COMPROMISED` status. |

For a future rotation from an eligible active key, approve a successor governance event naming the predecessor **key ID and exact prior pin identity**, successor key ID and public PEM hash, activation UTC, planned retirement UTC, and whether a short overlap is allowed. Create and independently pin the successor as above with the exact approved predecessor identity. During any approved overlap, both may verify, but each new signature names exactly one key ID and pin; the operator selects the currently approved signer. At cutover, stop signing with the predecessor, publish a newly sealed `RETIRED` pin for it, and independently pin both status changes. Old manifests and signatures remain byte-identical. Emergency rotation suspends signing immediately, records the reason/time, creates a successor on a clean device, publishes new external pins, and leaves the predecessor in `REVOKED` or `COMPROMISED` as evidence warrants. No automatic transfer of trust occurs merely because a local `predecessor_key_id` names a key. KEY-001 was ineligible and is not a predecessor in KEY-002's actual activation lineage.

If exposure is suspected, stop new signing and current acceptance under the suspect key pending governance review; preserve evidence, isolate the device/media, and initiate emergency rotation. Confirmed private-key or backup exposure means `COMPROMISED`. A lost device with no credible proof that the key remained protected is treated as suspected compromise. An altered pin or disagreeing external channels means **do not choose either locally**; stop until governance reconciles and independently re-pins the correct record. If compromise time cannot be independently established, do not claim any signature's original signing chronology. A later RFC 3161 timestamp can bound *manifest-identity* existence but cannot by itself prove when a private key signed or when corpus data was acquired. Preserve all old public evidence for investigation; never rewrite bundles.

## Timestamp and attrs readiness

An RFC 3161 token can independently establish that the stamped **manifest identity** existed no later than the TSA's verified `genTime`. It does not prove source acquisition date, rights, decision validity, or key custody. The current verifier **requires** a receipt and trusted CA for production-mode bundles and rejects missing or invalid timestamps. It checks TSA signature/chain and imprint; it does **not** independently validate request nonce, policy OID, or long-term revocation evidence when only a `.tsr` is supplied. Preserve the `.tsq`, nonce, policy, TSA CA identity, certificate/revocation material and verify those separately before making a stronger trusted-time claim. No production TSA request is made here. See [RFC 3161](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc3161.html).

The policy is `SIGNATURE_REQUIRED` plus `TIMESTAMP_REQUIRED`. DigiCert RFC 3161 Timestamp Authority is selected but has not been contacted; its trust-anchor preparation is independently retained, while no production response has been requested. Production-mode verification fails closed without a valid response and CA, while the offline verifier still grants `authority=NONE`. This timestamp check does not implement nonce/policy or archival revocation validation, and it does not authorize source use. Any emergency exception would need a separate bounded governance decision and implementation, never a silent fallback.

The attrs prospective decision `sha256:1771ad1444e577949ac6541d4fe166c7d229824954bd1d7c70a810ff5b58d754` is a separate approved intended-use input, not a signed production bundle.
